# Week 4 instructor notes

## Preparation

Ask students to bring a working project and its runbook. Prepare the inspection-log template and feedback cards.

## Demo idea

Improve a confusing empty state, then make completed tasks visually distinct without removing the ability to reopen them. Use feedback from actual use rather than a predetermined styling agenda.

## Instructor script and facilitation

Begin with a functioning baseline: add a task, complete it and check the count. Record this before requesting changes.

Say: “Useful feedback tells the agent what happened, what you expected, and what should stay working.” Demonstrate a task label that is hard to distinguish from its action button. Describe the actual confusion rather than prescribing a redesign of the entire interface.

At minute 20, the author stays quiet. The user receives a task such as “Find what Alex still needs to do.” The observer records pauses and misclicks without explaining them away.

After testing, let the student choose one issue by frequency and consequence. Ask whether success can be checked after the change. A preference can be a valid change, but label it as a preference rather than a defect.

During the first and second improvement blocks, have the agent change one behavior and preserve the baseline. If the agent proposes additional work, put it in a later list unless it is necessary for the accepted change.

At minute 70, deliberately revisit the original path. A change that adds a helpful filter but breaks task completion is not accepted. If the change does not help, keep the evidence and request a targeted correction.

## What to watch for

- Ask the learner to demonstrate: Translate user friction into specific feedback.
- Ask the learner to demonstrate: Make two bounded changes while preserving a known working path.
- Ask the learner to demonstrate: Use before/after evidence and regression checks to decide whether a change helped.

Use the same acceptance standard for Codex and Claude Code. If a tool-specific command is unavailable, use natural language and the current product's supported surface; do not invent a command.

## Debrief

Ask: “What did you decide? What did you observe? What remains uncertain?” Collect two bounded changes, a before/after log and an explicit list of deferred ideas.
